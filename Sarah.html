The Pull Temperature

RareMed-rareMediumMed-wellWell
Medium-Rare
On the plate: 135°F
Pull it at: 130°F
The lesson: a steak keeps cooking after it leaves the heat. That’s called carryover cooking, and it’s usually about 5°F. If you wait until the thermometer says your number, you’ve already overshot it. Pull early and let the rest finish the job.

Three Rules for a Steakhouse Crust at Home

Tap each card to flip it.

1
Salt it the night before
Tap to flip
Salt both sides and leave it uncovered in the fridge overnight. The salt seasons it all the way through, and the surface dries out. A dry surface browns. A wet one steams.
2
Hot pan, then butter
Tap to flip
Cast iron, smoking hot, a little high-heat oil. Flip every minute or so for an even crust. For the last minute, add butter, smashed garlic and thyme, tilt the pan and spoon it over the top.
3
Rest it, then slice it right
Tap to flip
Let it rest 5 to 10 minutes so the juices stay in the steak instead of on the cutting board. Then slice against the grain, across the lines in the meat, so every bite is tender.
